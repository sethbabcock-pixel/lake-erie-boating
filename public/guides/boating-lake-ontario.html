Boating Lake Ontario

By the shouldiboat.com team · Published November 6, 2026

Lake Ontario is the smallest of the Great Lakes by area but one of the deepest, and its shape makes it more dangerous than its size suggests. It runs long and narrow from west to east, so the prevailing westerly winds have the whole length of the lake to build waves. The launches we cover sit along the New York shore, from Olcott east to Sackets Harbor.

West winds and the eastern end

Lake Ontario stretches roughly 190 miles from the Niagara River in the west to the start of the St. Lawrence River in the east, but is only about 50 miles across at its widest. A west or southwest wind blows along that long axis and keeps building waves the whole way. By the time they reach the eastern end, around Oswego and Sackets Harbor, the seas can be large and long even in a wind that seems moderate.

North winds cross the lake's width and hit the New York shore head-on. They have less fetch than a westerly, but they blow straight into the harbor mouths along the south shore. A south wind is offshore along most of this coast: calm at the launch, rougher farther out. Each spot page lists the directions that are onshore and offshore at that launch; see wind direction & fetch.

River-mouth harbors between piers

Many south-shore harbors sit at river mouths between long piers or breakwalls: the Genesee River at Rochester, the Oswego River at Oswego, and Eighteenmile Creek at Olcott, among others. In an onshore wind, these entrances are often the roughest part of the trip:

  • Waves steepen as they reach the shallower water at the entrance and can break across it.
  • River current flowing out against incoming waves makes them steeper still, especially after heavy rain upstream.
  • Waves reflect off the pier walls and make the channel confused.

Look at the entrance before going out, and remember that you'll be coming back in with the waves behind you, which is harder to steer. If the sea is building, come back early rather than late.

Sheltered bays

The south shore has several bays that offer good boating when the open lake is too rough: Sodus Bay, Irondequoit Bay near Rochester, and the bays at the eastern end around Sackets Harbor, such as Black River Bay and Henderson Harbor. They're more protected than the open lake, though the eastern-end bays can still take a sea when a strong west or southwest wind blows into them, and their entrances are narrow channels where the lake's waves meet the bay. Check the entrance before heading out of the bay onto the lake, and know that the bay can be calm while the lake outside is not.

Ships and the Seaway

Lake Ontario is part of the route between the Atlantic and the upper Great Lakes, through the Welland Canal at the western end and the St. Lawrence Seaway at the eastern end. Large lake freighters and ocean ships cross the lake and approach the ports. They can't stop or turn quickly and may not see a small boat close ahead. Keep a lookout, stay clear of their path, and listen to VHF Channel 16 and 13 (see VHF radio and calling for help).

Cold water

Ontario is deep and stays cold late into spring. Offshore winds can trigger upwelling that brings cold water up to the surface near shore in hours, so a warm beach day can sit next to dangerously cold water. Wear your life jacket, dress for the water temperature, and if a spot page doesn't show a water temperature, assume it's cold. See cold-water safety.

Summer storms and fall gales

Summer thunderstorms can move off the land and across the lake quickly, often in the afternoon or evening, with sudden wind shifts and strong gusts (see thunderstorms and lightning). From October on, strong fall storms bring gales and big seas to the eastern end in particular. The offshore weather buoys are removed before winter; while they're out, spot pages use the NWS forecast for wind and waves and say so.

The short version: on Lake Ontario, a west wind has the whole lake to build waves, so the eastern end takes the biggest seas. The harbor entrance is often the roughest spot of the day, and the sheltered bays are your friend when the lake is up.
