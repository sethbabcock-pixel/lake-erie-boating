Launching and retrieving at the ramp

By the shouldiboat.com team · Published November 6, 2026

The boat ramp can be the most stressful part of the day: a line of trucks waiting, wind pushing the boat around, and a trailer that won't go where you point it. Almost all of that stress goes away with a routine. Do the work in the parking lot, keep your time on the ramp short, and make each step the same every time.

Before you get in line: the staging area

Most ramps have a staging or prep area. Use it to get the boat completely ready before you pull onto the ramp:

  • Put the drain plug in. It's the most common launch mistake, and a boat without its plug fills quickly. Make it the first thing on your checklist, and check it again.
  • Load gear, coolers and safety equipment, and put life jackets where everyone can reach them.
  • Remove the transom tie-downs and any travel cover, and raise the motor to clear the ramp.
  • Attach bow and stern lines so you can hold the boat once it floats.
  • Leave the bow winch strap and safety chain attached until the trailer is at the water's edge. A boat can slide off the trailer on a steep ramp.
  • Disconnect the trailer lights if your trailer uses older incandescent lights; hot bulbs can crack when dunked in cold water.
  • Check the weather and conditions for the day on your launch's spot page before you commit.

Backing down the ramp

Backing a trailer feels backwards because it is. The easy trick: put your hand on the bottom of the steering wheel, and move your hand in the direction you want the trailer to go. Make small corrections, go slowly, and use your mirrors. If it starts going wrong, pull forward, straighten out and start again; nobody minds a second try as much as a jackknifed trailer.

Practice in an empty parking lot first. A spotter outside the vehicle helps, as long as you agree on signals and they stay where you can see them and out from behind the trailer.

Launching

  1. Back down until the boat is close to floating. On most boats, that's when the trailer's fenders or the back of the hull are at the water.
  2. Put the vehicle in park, set the parking brake, and if the ramp is steep or slippery, have someone chock a wheel. Keep the driver in or near the vehicle.
  3. Hold a bow or stern line, unhook the winch strap and safety chain, and ease the boat off the trailer, or back down a little farther so it floats free.
  4. Walk the boat to the courtesy dock, or have someone aboard start the engine (after checking the blower and the water intake) and back it clear.
  5. Pull the vehicle and trailer out promptly and park in the trailer spaces.

Never stand between the boat and the trailer, or behind a boat sliding off a trailer, and keep hands clear of a winch under load: a slipping strap can spin the handle hard enough to break a wrist.

Wind and current at the ramp

A crosswind or current at the ramp pushes the boat sideways the moment it floats. Plan for it: keep a line on the upwind or up-current side, launch with the boat angled slightly into the wind or current if you can, and have a second person ready on the dock. If the wind is blowing straight onto the ramp with waves breaking on it, the ramp may be the hardest part of the day; that's a good time to reconsider going out at all.

Retrieving

  1. Tie up at the courtesy dock and fetch the vehicle and trailer, rather than circling in front of the ramp.
  2. Back the trailer in until the bunks or rollers are partly submerged. How deep depends on your trailer; note what works and do the same each time.
  3. Guide the boat onto the trailer slowly, centered between the guides, with lines or under low power. Some ramps prohibit power loading (driving the boat onto the trailer under engine power) because the prop wash digs holes at the end of the ramp; follow posted rules.
  4. Attach the winch strap and safety chain, winch the boat snug to the bow stop, and raise the motor.
  5. Pull clear of the ramp to the staging area before you unload, tie down, and do anything else.

Clean, drain, dry

Before you leave the ramp area, pull the drain plug, clean off weeds, mud and shells, and drain the bilge, livewells and motor. Invasive species such as zebra and quagga mussels and aquatic weeds spread from lake to lake on boats and trailers, and many states require boats to be cleaned and drained before leaving a launch. Let everything dry before launching in another body of water.

Ramp courtesy

  • Prep in the staging area, not on the ramp. Retrieve and tie down in the staging area, not on the ramp.
  • Take your turn and keep it moving; if you're waiting, offer a hand to the boat ahead.
  • Keep the courtesy dock clear for boats launching and retrieving, not for loading picnics.
  • Go slow near the ramp; your wake slams boats tied to the dock.

The short version: put the plug in, prep everything in the staging area, keep the strap on until you're at the water, set the parking brake, and keep your time on the ramp short. Clean, drain and dry before you leave.
