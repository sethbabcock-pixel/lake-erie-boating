Boating Southern California

By the shouldiboat.com team

Southern California boats year-round, but its water is colder and its swell bigger than the sunshine suggests. The daily rhythm is fairly predictable: calm, often foggy mornings, then a building afternoon breeze. A few seasonal wind and swell patterns are worth knowing before you leave the harbor.

The daily rhythm

Mornings usually start with light wind, often under a low marine layer of cloud or fog, especially in late spring and early summer. As the land warms, a westerly sea breeze builds through the afternoon and can reach 15 to 20 knots in exposed areas. Plan longer runs early and expect a choppier, wetter ride home into the afternoon wind. In fog, a chartplotter (radar if you have it) and a horn make a big difference.

Santa Ana winds

In fall and winter, high pressure inland sometimes sends hot, dry, gusty Santa Ana winds from the northeast, offshore. Near shore the water can look deceptively flat while the wind pushes small boats away from the coast and builds a steep chop farther out. Santa Anas can arrive quickly and gust well above the forecast average. When they're forecast, stay close to the harbor.

Swell season and harbor entrances

Winter brings the biggest long-period swells from the west and northwest; summer brings swell from the south. Long-period swell rides comfortably offshore, but it can break across shallow harbor entrances and jetties. Watch the entrance before going out and before coming back, especially at low tide. The NWS coastal forecast lists the swell height, period and direction for your zone.

Crossing to Catalina and the islands

Santa Catalina Island sits about 20 miles off the coast, across the San Pedro Channel, and the Channel Islands lie farther north and west. The crossing is open ocean. Go early, before the afternoon westerlies build; check the forecast for the channel, not just the harbor; and file a float plan. Popular island harbors use moorings, so check availability before you go.

Kelp and protected areas

  • Kelp beds can wrap a propeller. Go around them, and if you snag one, shut down before clearing it.
  • Marine Protected Areas line much of the coast and the islands, with limits on fishing and collecting. Check the state's MPA maps before you fish somewhere new.

Cold water

Coastal water temperatures in winter commonly sit in the upper 50s to low 60s Fahrenheit. That's cold enough for cold shock if you go in. Dress for the water, and wear your life jacket on the crossing.
